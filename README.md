# Dark matter spikes close to galactic black holes and their impact for DM Signals at telescopes
 
## Abstract
 
The study of dark matter has been one of the main topics in physics beyond the Standard Model over the past decades.
To date, considerable astrophysical evidence points to the existence of additional non-baryonic matter, known as dark matter, whose nature has not yet been determined.
The leading candidates are WIMPs, hypothetical particles interacting through the gravitational and weak interactions, whose abundance is naturally explained by the thermal relic mechanism.
 
The aim of this thesis is to study the impact of the uncertainties on the dark matter density profile of the Milky Way on the indirect detection constraints obtained from the Galactic Center, the most promising target for this kind of search.
We introduce a set of representative profiles, fixing their parameters through observational constraints and computing the corresponding J-factors, which in the central region vary by more than two orders of magnitude across the different profiles.
A simple thermal relic model is then compared with the experimental limits on the annihilation cross section to assess their compatibility.
 
Moreover, the Galactic Center hosts the supermassive black hole Sgr A*, whose adiabatic growth can redistribute the surrounding dark matter into a very dense cusp, known as a spike.
In this context, two benchmarks are defined: the Gondolo–Silk (GS) spike and a version softened by stellar heating (LSH).
The comparison of the thermal relic model with the spiked density profiles clearly shows that the observational constraints depend critically on the inner dark matter distribution:
in the GS benchmark, dark matter with a thermal cross section is excluded for masses above about 3.5 TeV, whereas in the LSH case it remains allowed at all masses.
